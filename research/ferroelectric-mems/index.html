Ferroelectric MEMS

I connect nonlinear material modelling with device-scale simulation and experimental characterization of ferroelectric thin-film MEMS. Inverse identification links measured electromechanical response to material parameters.

OverviewSelected work 4

My work on ferroelectric MEMS connects nonlinear material modelling with device-scale simulation and experiment. I have developed phenomenological continuum formulations for ferroelectric, antiferroelectric, and antiferroelectric-like materials, and reduced actuator models that can be calibrated against measured electromechanical response. Laser Doppler vibrometry has been used to characterize electrically driven microstructures and to connect constitutive behaviour with observable device dynamics.

This direction also includes inverse identification from experimental data. I have combined physics-based models with physics-informed neural networks to identify electromechanical parameters from limited observations, and with Fourier neural operators to represent history-dependent ferroelectric hysteresis. Current work extends this progression toward differentiable finite-element simulation, where forward analysis and parameter sensitivities are treated within the same computational framework.

Selected work

  1. 01 2026 · Preprint and submitted manuscript

    Differentiable finite elements for history-dependent ferroelectricity: matrix-free forward analysis and adjoint inverse identification

    A differentiable finite-element framework for nonlinear ferroelectric MEMS that keeps the local switching problem, global electromechanical equilibrium, and loading history inside one forward/inverse model. The formulation combines implicit differentiation, matrix-free nonlinear solution, and a fully discrete adjoint for trajectory-based parameter identification.

    Simplified phenomenological model for ferroelectric micro-actuator

    A thermodynamically based phenomenological model connects ferroelectric switching to coupled finite-element actuator simulation and device-scale characterization. The associated micro-cantilever work links the material description to LDV measurements and ferroelectric thin-film characterization.

    Computational modeling of uniaxial antiferroelectric and antiferroelectric-like actuators

    The ferroelectric modelling line is extended to antiferroelectric and antiferroelectric-like switching, translating pinched and double P-E hysteresis into a practical constitutive update and coupled actuator model.

    Fourier neural operator-based modeling of ferroelectric device under large signal and high frequency

    Experimentally measured voltage-displacement trajectories across amplitudes and frequencies are used to train a Fourier neural operator for nonlinear ferroelectric micro-actuation, extending operator learning toward large-signal excitation, high frequency, and structural resonance.
